LESSON TITLELevels of Classification and Biological Taxonomy

Declaration of AI useThis lesson plan was generated with the assistance of AI to structure activities according to DepEd pedagogical standards.
INTENTIONS Meaningful learning experiences are anchored in how we frame them.
Curriculum StandardsThe learners demonstrate an understanding of the concept of a species and the hierarchical system of classification of organisms.
Learning CompetencyExplain the hierarchical system of classification of organisms (Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species) and the rules of binomial nomenclature (Competency-aligned statement).
Learning ObjectivesAt the end of the 4-session module, learners will be able to: 1. Describe the eight taxonomic ranks in biological classification; 2. Apply the rules of binomial nomenclature when writing scientific names; 3. Use a simple dichotomous key to identify and classify local Philippine organisms; and 4. Recognize the importance of systematic classification in biodiversity conservation.
Learner ContextGrade 8 public high school learners with mixed academic readiness, working in small groups with access to print modules, visual charts, and localized plant/animal picture cards.
LEARNING EXPERIENCES A learning experience is like a thoughtfully designed journey.
Learning ExperienceSession 1Session 2Session 3Session 4
Date / Time
Pre-LessonConduct a 5-minute activity called 'Organize the Supermarket' where students group 10 everyday items into logical categories and explain their rationale.Quick recall game: 'Taxonomic Ladder' where student representatives arrange jumbled cards of the taxonomic levels from broadest to most specific.Present pictures of two similar-looking local plants (e.g., Sampaguita and Chichirica) and ask students how a scientist would distinguish them.Peer check: Groups exchange and verify their completed dichotomous key identification worksheets from Wednesday.
FlowIntroduce Taxonomy and Carolus Linnaeus. Discuss the concept of classifying biological diversity and present the 8 hierarchical levels (Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species) using the mnemonic 'Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup'.Discuss Binomial Nomenclature rules (Genus capitalized, species lowercase, italicized or underlined). Guide students in writing scientific names of local organisms such as Panthera pardus, Oryza sativa, and Jasminum sambac.Introduce simple Dichotomous Keys. Guide student groups in navigating a 4-step dichotomous key to identify five Philippine endemic organisms (e.g., Tamaraw, Philippine Eagle, Tarsier, Milkfish, Narra tree). Safety rule: Handle specimen cards carefully and maintain orderly group discussion.Facilitate a class synthesis connecting taxonomy to biodiversity conservation. Administer a 10-item multiple-choice formative quiz covering taxonomic ranks, scientific naming, and dichotomous key usage.
Learning ResourcesTaxonomy graphic organizer, Manila paper, visual diagram of taxonomic hierarchy.Binomial nomenclature rule chart, practice worksheets, whiteboard markers.Dichotomous key activity sheets, picture cards of Philippine flora and fauna.Formative assessment sheets, summary slides/visual chart.
Opportunities for integrationValues Integration: Orderliness, systematic thinking, and organization in daily life.Language Arts: Applying proper capitalization, typography rules, and Latin root word awareness.Environmental Education & Nationalism: Appreciating endemic Philippine species and biodiversity hotspots.Critical Thinking: Evaluating how accurate scientific naming aids global conservation efforts and medical research.
ASSESSING LEARNING
Formative AssessmentDaily exit cards, group worksheet outputs on dichotomous keys, peer-reviewed scientific naming drills, and a 10-item synthesis quiz.
WAYS FORWARD
Extended learning opportunitiesCreate a localized mini-catalog of five schoolyard or neighborhood organisms including their common names, local names, and properly written scientific names.
